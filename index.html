Every life has value. Every person deserves hope. Everyone deserves a second chance.

Across Fiji, vulnerable children, young people and adults face homelessness, poverty, family breakdown, addiction and social exclusion. At Seek to Save Ministry, we believe no one should be forgotten or left behind.

Founded by Elia Berabi, Seek to Save is a Christian outreach ministry based in Colo-i-Suva, Fiji, dedicated to reaching people who are vulnerable, marginalised and in need of compassion, support and a renewed sense of purpose.

Our mission is simple: To reach the forgotten, restore dignity and help transform lives through the love of Jesus Christ.

Our Story

From a Simple Act of Compassion to a Mission of Hope

“I was a stranger and you invited me in.” Matthew 25:35

Seek to Save Ministry began with a simple conviction: people living on the streets deserve more than to be passed by. In 2023, Elia Berabi and his younger brother encountered children and young people sleeping beneath a bridge near the Suva Fish Market. They began reaching out with food, conversation, compassion, prayer and by sharing the word of God.

As they listened, they encountered stories affected by homelessness, broken family relationships, abuse, poverty, methamphetamine addiction and HIV infection. Elia and his family began welcoming vulnerable young people into their home in Colo-i-Suva, offering food, shelter, guidance and belonging. What began as an act of kindness grew into Seek to Save Ministry.

A Family That Opened Its Home

Despite limited resources, Elia and his family have offered practical support, spiritual guidance and opportunities for young people to rebuild their lives. Agricultural activities, including dalo farming, encourage discipline, teamwork, practical skills and self-reliance.

A Full House

Today, 24 people share one bedroom, one living room and a kitchen area. Every week, new requests arrive for a place in the home, and with no room left they have to be turned down. Each refusal is painful, and it is why the vision of a dedicated facility matters so much. Read about our building project

Growing Beyond the Family Home

The ministry aspires to establish a dedicated care and rehabilitation facility, supported by appropriate professional services, offering safe accommodation, structured programmes and personal development. Our vision is not simply to provide a roof overhead, but to help people discover that their past does not have to define their future.

Fiji's government declared an HIV outbreak in January 2025 and, in September 2026, declared it a national crisis. Health authorities link much of the spread to injecting methamphetamine and sharing needles, and to a shortage of treatment and support services.

The figures above are reported cases only. Because fear, shame and limited access to testing keep many people from being tested, the true number of people living with HIV in Fiji is believed to be higher than the numbers reported.

Methamphetamine is now widely available in Fiji, and young people are among those most affected. Fear and shame stop many people from being tested or seeking help, yet HIV is treatable, and treatment in Fiji is free.

Farming & Sustainability

Growing food for daily needs

“Let us not become weary in doing good, for at the proper time we will reap a harvest if we do not give up.” Galatians 6:9

Alongside outreach and care, the ministry grows dalo and other crops to help meet daily needs. Farming is also a way of teaching: young people learn patient, practical skills, and the harvest may in time become a source of income.

Reports in early 2026 described a dalo planting of around 35,000 plants. With additional tools, seeds, water access and agricultural support, this work could contribute to food security and lasting livelihoods.

Testimonies

Lives being restored, in their own words

Each testimony is shared by the person themselves, or with their informed consent, and told with dignity.

“Come and hear, all you who fear God; let me tell you what he has done for me.” Psalm 66:16

How we protect people. Nothing is published until the person and the ministry have approved it in writing. Names may be shortened or changed, faces are not shown by default, and testimonies from children are not published.

How a testimony is added

  1. Invitation
    The person is invited to share, and is free to say no.
  2. Written consent
    They agree in writing to the words, name and any photograph.
  3. Safety review
    The ministry checks that nothing could identify or endanger anyone.
  4. Publication
    The testimony is published with a date and can be withdrawn at any time.

Policies

How we protect people and gifts

“To act justly and to love mercy and to walk humbly with your God.” Micah 6:8

Privacy

Donor and contact details are handled securely and used only for the purpose you give them. Consent is requested before updates are sent.

Safeguarding

We do not publish identifying information, locations, diagnoses or exploitation histories of vulnerable residents without lawful, informed approval. For children we apply especially protective standards and avoid identifiable stories by default.

Health information and confidentiality

We never disclose anyone's health status, including HIV status, drug use or treatment, without their informed consent. We do not tolerate stigma or discrimination against people living with HIV, and we encourage free, voluntary and confidential testing and treatment.

Donations & refunds

Daily Care and Build a Home gifts are recorded separately. Refund terms and the handling of restricted gifts, including what happens if the building project changes, will be stated here before giving opens. We do not describe gifts as tax-deductible unless independently confirmed.

Financial accountability

We will name who manages donations, publish financial updates and report on the use of funds as milestones are approved.

Accessibility

This site uses clear headings, readable contrast, keyboard navigation and descriptive image text.
